## The one-liner

**"Hour Keeper is the simplest way for a field tech to record their hours against the job, and the first time the office can see what a job really took against what we sold it for."**

## The 30-second version

Today, what we sell a job for and what it takes in the field live in different places and don't meet until the job is over, if ever. Hour Keeper puts one simple screen on a tech's phone: tap the task, tap the hours, done. It works with no signal. The PM sees sold against actual, task by task, without chasing anyone. Because it's built to help the tech first, people use it, and that's what makes the data good enough to learn from.

---

## 1. The problem

**What the office sees**
- We quote hours by task, but we only find out how a job really went when it's finished, if at all.
- When a job runs over, we can't say which task, and we can't tell whether it was the quote, the site, or a variation.
- The same tasks may run over on job after job, but nothing links jobs together, so quotes don't improve.
- Hours reach the office late, in different formats, and need re-typing. **[add your real example, e.g. "On Highett we found the sheet total said 193 but the lines added to 216"]**

**What the field sees**
- Timesheets and checklists feel like paperwork that nobody reads.
- Apps that need signal fail in shafts, basements and new builds.
- Anything that looks like tracking makes people nervous, and then they stop using it honestly.

## 2. The idea: simple enough that people want to use it

The design rule from the start: **if a tech doesn't find it helpful, the data is worthless.**

| Rule | What it means in the app |
|---|---|
| **Fewest taps possible** | Tap the task, tap the hours, Save. A day's entry takes seconds. |
| **Works with no signal** | Everything saves on the phone first and sends later. Shafts, basements and remote sites are fine. |
| **Techs see only their job** | The job they're assigned to, the tasks, their own hours. Nothing else. |
| **No spying features** | No GPS, no clock-in/clock-out, no live monitoring, no alerts, no league tables. |
| **Straight hours for the field** | Techs and supervisors see hours. Percentages and comparisons are for PMs, after the fact, not live. |
| **Honest edits, both ways** | If an entry is corrected, it shows "Edited", and everyone can see what changed and who changed it. Nobody's hours are silently changed. |
| **It gives something back** | Photos, notes and comments record the site problems: a missing pit ladder, late builder, a variation. That protects the tech and backs up the variation claim. |

## 3. What the office gets

- **Sold vs actual, task by task**, as the job goes, not weeks later.
- **Extras shown separately**: variations (VOs) and non-productive time such as site readiness, with the notes and photos that explain them.
- **Job comparison for PMs**: which tasks run over again and again across jobs. That is evidence for re-quoting.
- **One-tap PDF report** to share, from inside the app.
- **Jobs set up from the hours sheets you already have** (Excel, CSV or PDF), checked on one screen before saving.
- **No training course.** A new tech can be using it the same day.

## 4. Where it is now

- Built and working: job setup, importing hours sheets, assigning techs, logging hours offline, photos and comments, edit history, reports, PM comparison, PDF sharing.
- Tested with a real job's data as the demo (**[Highett, 59 tasks, 216 hours]**).
- Running as a web app that installs on a phone's home screen.
- A small beta is next: **[number]** people on **[one job]** for 2–3 weeks.

> Don't oversell this. Present it as a working prototype, not a finished enterprise product. Saying "here's what we've learned from real use" is far more convincing than claiming it's ready for everyone.

## 5. The ask: a small, low-risk pilot

**Not** "adopt this globally". Ask for:

- **Scope:** one branch or region, **[3–5]** jobs, **[10–20]** techs, **[8–12 weeks]**.
- **Cost to Schindler:** time to run the pilot and an IT/security review. **[Hosting is minimal for a pilot, e.g. around US$25/month; confirm before quoting.]**
- **What we'll measure (agree these up front):**
  - **Adoption:** do at least ~80% of assigned techs log hours most days?
  - **Time:** do techs say it takes under a minute a day?
  - **Data quality:** how many hours land on "Other" lines, and how many entries are corrected?
  - **Office value:** did the PM spot an over-run earlier than they otherwise would have? Did a quote change as a result?
  - **Feedback:** what the field and the office say, in their own words.
- **Decision at the end:** continue, expand, change, or stop. A clear stop option makes it easier to say yes.

## 6. Questions head office will ask (and honest answers)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Isn't this just tracking our people?"** | No. No GPS, clock-in, live monitoring or rankings. Techs see their own hours. Percentages are for PMs and only after the fact. The pilot should also be agreed with the field and any employee representatives up front. |
| **"Don't we already have systems for this?"** | **[Check this before the meeting.]** If an existing system covers task-level sold-vs-actual hours that the field actually uses, say how this complements it. The pilot answers it: does the field use this one, and does it produce data the office couldn't get before? |
| **"What about data security and privacy?"** | Data sits in a database with row-level access rules, so techs only see their own jobs. Photos are in a private store. For a company rollout this would need Schindler's own IT review and hosting. See the section below. |
| **"Will it connect to our systems (SAP / payroll)?"** | Not yet. It's on the roadmap, and the data is structured for it. The pilot shows whether it's worth connecting. |
| **"Can it scale to thousands of users and many countries?"** | The design supports it (branches, regions, languages, time zones) but it hasn't been tested at that scale. That's what a staged rollout is for. |
| **"Who maintains it?"** | This needs an honest answer before the meeting: pilot support from you, and a proposal for IT ownership if it grows. |
| **"What if the phones have no signal?"** | It works fully offline and sends later. |

## 9. Two-minute spoken pitch

"On every job we sell hours by task. But what actually happens on site only reaches the office late, in different formats, and often never gets compared with the quote. So when a job runs over, we can't say which task, and next quote we make the same mistake.

Techs don't avoid timesheets because they're lazy. They avoid them because the tools are slow, don't work in a lift shaft, and feel like they're watching them. So I built the opposite. Hour Keeper is a phone app where you tap the task, tap the hours and you're done. It works with no signal. Techs see only their own job. There's no GPS, no clock-in, no live monitoring. If a PM corrects an entry, it's marked Edited, and everyone can see what changed.

And it gives something back to the tech: they can attach a photo or a note when there's a problem on site, like a missing pit ladder. That protects them, and it backs up a variation claim.

For the office, the payoff is a clear sold-versus-actual report for every job, with variations and non-productive time shown separately, and a view across jobs that shows which tasks run over again and again. That's the evidence to improve how we quote.

It's built and working with a real job's data. I'm not asking for a global rollout. I'm asking for a short pilot, [X] jobs in [region] over [Y] weeks, with success measures we agree up front, and a clear decision at the end."

## Before you send anything: for you, not for the slides

1. **Get your own manager's approval first.** Going to Switzerland over their head can hurt you. The strongest version of this has **[your manager / regional manager]** presenting it with you, or at least knowing about it.
2. **The app currently runs on your own accounts** (Supabase, Vercel, GitHub), not Schindler's. For a pilot that's fine, but **don't put real customer or job data in it without checking with your manager and IT first.** Schindler almost certainly has rules on where company data may be stored, and the Highett drawings carry an IP notice. A head-office pitch will immediately ask where the data sits, and you want to answer "I checked, and here's the plan" rather than "I hadn't thought about it".
3. **Ask who owns it.** Because you built it, check your employment terms and your manager's expectations about who owns the app, and make sure that's clear before the pitch.
4. **Check what already exists.** Schindler likely has field and time systems in some regions. Find out what they are so you can say how this differs or complements. Nothing kills a pitch faster than "we already have that".
5. **Prepare the field story.** If you can bring one or two techs' honest quotes after a beta week, e.g. "it takes me under a minute", that is worth more than any slide.
6. **Be careful with numbers.** Don't claim savings you haven't measured. "Here's what we'll measure" is more credible than "this will save X".
